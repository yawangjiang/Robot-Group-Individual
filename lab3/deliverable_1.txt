Required headers for the examples:
#include <tf2_geometry_msgs/tf2_geometry_msgs.h>
#include <tf2/utils.h>
#include <tf2_eigen/tf2_eigen.h>
#include <Eigen/Geometry>

A. Message vs. tf

A1. Convert an incoming geometry_msgs::Quaternion quat_msg to the already
    declared tf2::Quaternion quat_tf:

tf2::fromMsg(quat_msg, quat_tf);

A2. Convert tf2::Quaternion quat_tf to a newly declared ROS quaternion message:

geometry_msgs::Quaternion quat_msg = tf2::toMsg(quat_tf);

A3. Read the scalar component of the tf2 quaternion:

quat_tf.getW();

B. Conversion

B1. Extract the yaw component in one function call (radians):

double yaw = tf2::getYaw(quat_t);

B2. Convert geometry_msgs::Quaternion quat_msg to an Eigen 3-by-3 matrix:

Eigen::Quaterniond quat_eigen;
tf2::fromMsg(quat_msg, quat_eigen);
Eigen::Matrix3d R = quat_eigen.toRotationMatrix();

The two conversion calls are tf2::fromMsg and toRotationMatrix. The first
overload is supplied by tf2_eigen. A finite unit quaternion is assumed; a
nonzero non-unit input should be normalized before constructing R.
